# Pundits want reasons, not just charts for MatchMind

Live football coverage, as critics describe it, spends its time on personality, anecdote and betting shorthand, and under-explains the tactical shape of the game. The clearest tactical gaps are positional shape changes between possession and out of possession, pressing triggers, full-back overloads and the effect of substitutions. The broadcast graphics and consumer apps that exist mostly show *what happened* (heatmaps, shot maps, xG, basic pass networks), not *why it happened* or *what would have changed*. MatchMind's core idea of a tactical reason per event is aimed at a gap the notes support, but the notes contain no comprehension study that proves viewers understand tactical graphics better with it. The strongest case for MatchMind is therefore explanation-first design with honest uncertainty, not novelty for its own sake. Most of the features the brief lists already exist in the prototype (3D pitch, heatmap, pressure timeline, what-if pitch, recap, agent trace). The next value lies in making each explanation clearer, checkable and calibrated, and in the viewer-facing copy that carries it. This report ranks the gaps, maps viewer segments and competitors to them, and ends with a prioritised build list for the five MatchMind modules.

## Pundit critiques rank personality and anecdote as the top gap

The most frequent complaint in the sources is that studio punditry replaces tactical substance with recognisable faces and generic star talk. A 2017 critique argues that only a small share of a co-commentator's work is insight a non-player could not know, with most of that being anecdote ([The Boar, 2017](https://theboar.org/2017/04/critique-critics-football-punditry/)). A Football365 piece argues that producers hire former players and assume they make the best pundits ([Football365](https://www.football365.com/news/ex-footballer-pundit-culture-john-nicholson)). A US critic says coverage gives generic lines about star players rather than tactics or likely mismatches ([Awaydayz](https://awaydayz.substack.com/p/americas-soccer-media-is-failing)). These are opinion pieces, so the finding is **a consistent critical theme, not a measured airtime share**. No 2026 broadcaster audit of tactical versus non-tactical airtime was found. The counter-view, that co-commentators teach something every week, was reported only in search results and was not verified.

The second gap is shape change between phases. The Premier League's own coaching analysis from September 2024 describes Brighton under Fabian Hurzeler setting up as a 4-2-3-1 without the ball and moving to a 4-1-2-3 with it ([Premier League Coaching Insights](https://www.premierleague.com/news/4122164)). This is the kind of change a viewer rarely sees explained live. Gary Neville's breakdowns, as summarised in search results, follow the same pattern of criticising defensive shape and off-ball movement, though those pieces were not fetched and should be re-checked before citing.

The third and fourth gaps are pressing triggers and substitutions. The same official analysis describes Aston Villa pressing as a block of four, with Ollie Watkins and Morgan Rogers blocking the deep midfielders and John McGinn's press triggering the others ([Premier League Coaching Insights](https://www.premierleague.com/news/4122164)). It also describes Bournemouth recovering from 2-0 down at Everton after Andoni Iraola's five changes between minutes 65 and 77 (same source). Both are concrete, named examples of explanations that a single live caption rarely gives.

Two figures from the same official piece, fewer high turnovers (16.87 to 14.86 per match) and PPDA moving from 12.84 to 11.59, are reported in the research notes as linked to teams blocking the middle third. The notes do not show these were checked against the page itself, so treat them as **secondhand until verified**.

The fifth gap is betting framing. Commentary notes that studio pundits increasingly frame matches in probability and value terms ([Betting Pundits](https://www.bettingpundits.com/football-pundits-increasingly-shape-uk-betting-conversations/)). This is a secondary, commercial source, and it is the weakest of the top five. It matters for MatchMind mainly as a reason to keep any probability output framed as information, not advice.

Counterfactuals form the sixth gap, and the one that matters most for MatchMind's what-if feature. Pundits often judge a decision by its outcome. Sources on hindsight bias advise judging the decision by its logic and reconstructing the alternative, but these are a blog and a 2021 fantasy-football article ([Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/6469397-cognitive-biases-in-football-punditry); [Fantasy Football Scout, 2021](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2021/05/20/hindsight-bias-and-how-not-to-look-back-in-anger-in-fpl)). No academic study of pundit counterfactual claims was found.

The last gap, and the least evidenced, is whether viewers understand the graphics at all. No peer-reviewed or industry study was found that measures which tactical concepts viewers misread, or how well replays and tactical boards work. Rankings below are based on how often sources raise each gap, which is approximate and weighted toward what could be retrieved.

| Rank | Gap | Evidence strength | Source type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tactics reduced to personality, anecdote or generic talk | Strong theme, but all opinion | Critics, trade press |
| 2 | Shape changes between possession and out of possession not explained | Moderate | Official PL analysis, Neville-style breakdowns |
| 3 | Pressing triggers and blocks not explained live | Moderate | Official PL analysis |
| 4 | Substitution impact and why it mattered | Moderate | Official PL analysis (one match example) |
| 5 | Probability and betting framing crowding out tactics | Weak to moderate | Secondary commercial source |
| 6 | Counterfactuals judged by outcome | Lower frequency | Blog, 2021 article |
| 7 | Full-back overloads and off-ball movement | Lower | Named in official analysis, rarely framed as a viewer problem |
| 8 | Viewer comprehension of graphics | Unresolved | No evidence found |

## Viewers want the story and the reason, and most data goes unused

Viewer segments differ in what they want, and the research notes give more evidence on the segments' behaviour than on their comprehension. Casual fans most likely want the score-state, a few plain turning points and a short "why did that happen" for key moments. Analysts and die-hard fans want per-player and per-event explanation and tolerate more jargon. Fantasy and betting players want player-level stats and near-live updates, which is a licensing question as much as a design one. Second-screen and younger viewers favour short clips and mobile-first recaps over long dashboards.

The behaviour evidence is thinner than it looks. A secondhand article cites a **YouGov 2025 figure that 68% of soccer fans regularly look up statistics during games**, with no sample size or date given in the secondary source ([CaughtOffside, July 2026](https://www.caughtoffside.com/2026/07/08/how-football-fans-fill-the-time-between-matches/)). The same article cites **an IBM figure of 90% checking content beyond the live broadcast and about 61% watching highlights**, with no sample given. Treat all three as **unverified until the original releases are found**. A Gulf survey reported in trade press gives roughly 9.5 phone checks per match for UAE fans and 8.5 for Saudi fans ([OfficePoolStop summary](https://officepoolstop.com/blog/how-premier-league-fans-are-changing-their-second-screen)), but the original survey was not located. An Irish survey gives 25% of 15 to 24 year olds against 8% of over-55s who would use a second device, but it is older and not from 2025 ([Sport for Business](https://sportforbusiness.com/irelands-second-screen-sports-fans-revealed/)). None of these figures says what viewers skip, so the skip patterns in this report are inference.

For formats, the sources favour simple heatmaps and labelled timelines over coordinate lists. A general sports-broadcast piece says heatmaps are easier to follow than lists of coordinates for casual viewers, but it cites no audience data ([Grand Prix 247, 2025](https://www.grandprix247.com/2025/08/25/the-importance-of-data-visualization-in-sports-broadcasting/)). Fan guides document real confusion: a bright heatmap spot shows presence, not impact, and pass networks show players at average positions, not where they actually passed ([Lazada viewing guide](https://footballkit.lazada.sg/viewing-guide/how-to-read-heat-maps-pass/)). A pass network link is drawn only where a pair has 13 or more successful passes in one Soccermatics lesson, a threshold viewers would not see without a note ([Soccermatics](https://soccermatics.readthedocs.io/en/main/lesson1/VisualisingFootball.html)). These are **guides and examples, not comprehension tests**.

Accessibility guidance is clearer and transfers directly. Label data on the chart rather than relying on a legend, add shape or pattern to colour, keep non-text contrast at 3:1, and write short alt text that states the message ([UK Government Analysis Function](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-colours-in-charts); [WCAG contrast guidance](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/support/communicating-analysis/introduction-to-data-visualisation-e-learning/data-visualisation-e-learning-module-3-accessibility-and-colour-palettes)). No football-specific study tested whether these help comprehension, so the case rests on general practice.

## The competitive field has charts but almost no explanation

Consumer apps have commoditised the chart layer. Sofascore and FotMob list heatmaps, shot maps and xG as standard, and FotMob places xG and shot maps behind its paid tier, per its App Store listing ([FotMob App Store](https://apple.co/3mWC5mI)). The deeper, event-linked views sit in analyst platforms. StatsBomb's Pitch Vis, in a September 2026 Hudl release, highlights the event currently playing on the pitch and offers filters by outcome, body part, OBV and xG range ([Hudl StatsBomb release](https://www.hudl.com/releases/statsbomb/2026-09-01)). StatsBomb's 360 freeze frames show player positions at each event ([StatsBomb blog archive, 2021](https://blogarchive.statsbomb.com/news/statsbomb-360-freeze-frame-viewer-a-new-release-in-statsbomb-iq/)). Opta Vision, launched in 2022, lists outputs such as third-man runs, shape changes with and without the ball, and how pressure affects outcomes ([SBC News, 2022](https://sbcnews.co.uk/technology/2022/06/13/stats-perform-launches-opta-vision-for-new-generation-of-sports-data/)). These vendor claims have no independent accuracy evaluation in the notes.

Two points stand out. First, the what-if gap is real in products but **unverified as a whole**. No commercial product found offers interactive position-swap counterfactuals, but the search could not rule out a small hidden feature, and vendor sites were not searched for that wording. The academic basis is a November 2025 preprint that reallocates shots between players, using 9,970 StatsBomb 2015/16 shots and Football Manager priors ([arXiv 2511.23072](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23072v1)). Its illustrative swaps (about 2.2 xG added in one, about 7 xG dropped in another) are **from a preprint whose case studies are not tested against outcomes**, so they should not be quoted as findings. Second, auto-recaps are a vendor and broadcaster feature. Opta-powered insights reached Sky's pre-match and Monday Night Football packages, per a Stats Perform workshop ([Stats Perform](https://www.statsperform.com/insights/the-premier-league-is-back-the-opta-insights-powering-the-new-season/)), but no quality review of any auto-recap was found.

Consumer complaints centre on ads for Sofascore and paywalls for FotMob, based on aggregator review summaries ([Unstar 2026 comparison](https://unstar.app/blog/fotmob-onefootball-espn-365scores-sofascore-football-score-apps-ranked-2026)). These are weak evidence, since no Reddit threads or store reviews were read directly. The official Premier League app shows scores, alerts and text commentary, but no tactical visualisation was listed ([Premier League beginners guide](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4365156/premier-leagues-beginners-guide-all-you-need-to-know)). The Premier League's Microsoft partnership plans a fan-facing assistant covering 30+ seasons of stats ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4338015/premier-league-announces-partnership-with-microsoft)), which is a direct competitor on conversational explanation, though no product detail was verified. The open position is an explanation-first product with cited evidence and a calibrated what-if, not a richer chart set.

Licensing is the main barrier for any real-data product. Stats Perform does not publish pricing, and aggregator figures of $500 to $1,000+ per month are unverified ([Stats Perform MLA](https://www.statsperform.com/legal/mla/)). Opta data needs a commercial licence for commercial use ([SportsFirst](https://www.sportsfirst.net/sportsapi/opta-sports-api)). For a hackathon, synthetic data avoids this entirely, but any claim about real matches does not.

## Build these MatchMind improvements in priority order

The existing prototype is strong on the visual layer. The improvements below target the explanation layer, where the evidence gaps are largest and the cost to build is lowest. Each item is feasible on synthetic data unless marked otherwise.

| Priority | Improvement | MatchMind module | Why (gap it addresses) | Needs real data or licence? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Per-event reason card that names the shape phase (in or out of possession) and the trigger, with a one-line glossary tooltip for xG, pressure and overload | agents (Tactics, Narrator), app | Gaps 1 to 3; the most-cited pundit complaint | No |
| 2 | What-if panel that shows a range, names the model ("shot-value heuristic, context fixed"), and states what the range means in plain language. Avoid "would have won" | agents (Counterfactual), charts (what-if pitch), app | Gap 6; keeps the feature credible and avoids implied precision | No |
| 3 | Calibration check on the synthetic holdout: compare the heuristic's predicted shot values with simulated outcomes and publish the result beside the feature | engine (generator seed), agents | Makes the counterfactual falsifiable, as the notes recommend | No |
| 4 | Pressure timeline with the definition on screen and labelled turning points; press-trigger markers on the 3D pitch | charts (pressure timeline), pitch3d | Gaps 2 and 3 | No |
| 5 | Substitution card: show the swap, the minute, and the score-state before and after, with the reason it mattered | engine (add substitution events if absent), agents, app | Gap 4 | Needs substitution events added to the generator |
| 6 | Pass network and heatmap captions that state the threshold and say heatmaps show presence, not impact | charts | Confusion sources in fan guides | No |
| 7 | Accessibility pass: direct labels, shape or pattern as well as team colour, 3:1 contrast on pitch markings, generated alt text for each chart | charts, pitch3d, styles.css (app) | General guidance; cheapest wins | No |
| 8 | Recap that cites the event IDs behind each claim, so every line can be traced | agents (Narrator), app | Vendor recaps have no quality evidence; citing evidence is a differentiator | No |
| 9 | Clip-style "why this moment" list for producers, with reason, values and an approve or reject control | agents, app | Producer requirement: ranked moments with a reason | No for prototype; footage rights for distribution |
| 10 | Probability or value framing kept as information only, with a disclaimer; no betting advice | app | Betting-framing gap; regulatory and advice risk | No |

Items 1 to 4 fit the existing architecture without new data sources, and they answer the top three gaps directly. Items 5 and 9 need small generator changes or a clearer producer workflow. Real-data work, such as licensed Opta or StatsBomb feeds, should wait until the synthetic version has been tested with people. That test would also settle the biggest open question in this report.

## Evidence limits and what would change the picture

No comprehension study of tactical graphics, heatmaps, pass networks, shot maps or pressure timelines was found in any source, so none of the format rankings here is proven for football audiences. The what-if competitive gap is unverified, because the search did not check vendor sites for that feature and a hidden feature cannot be ruled out. Viewer trust in counterfactual graphics was not studied either. The 68%, 90% and 61% figures, the Gulf phone-check counts and the vendor claims (Sofascore, Spiideo, Zentag, Driblab, Grabyo, Eluvio) are **secondhand or self-reported and should not be cited as fact** until the originals are found. Pundit airtime and Premier League broadcast latency were not found. Sky, TNT and Amazon coverage was not verified at all. The Premier League's own data-rights restrictions on third-party use were not confirmed.

The fastest way to close these gaps is small: find the original YouGov and IBM releases, read the Sofascore and FotMob paywall in-app, run a five-person comprehension check on the MatchMind reason card against a plain caption, and check StatsBomb open-data licence terms before any real-data prototype.

## Conclusion

The gap that matters most for MatchMind is not missing charts. It is the absence of a short, checkable reason attached to each tactical moment, which pundits themselves say is what they lack, and which the consumer apps have not built. MatchMind's existing architecture already produces the raw material, so the next step is to make each explanation name its shape phase, its trigger and its uncertainty, and to publish how well the counterfactual is calibrated. That combination is an open position in the sources reviewed, though the evidence for it is strongest as a design argument rather than a measured viewer benefit.
