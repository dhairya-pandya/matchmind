The gap

Charts show what happened. Heatmaps, shot maps and xG are standard in consumer apps.

Pundits explain why. The best of them name the shape, the press and the substitution that mattered.

Few tools test the alternative. What if a winger had started wide, or a midfielder had dropped deeper? Almost none show the answer, or how sure it is.

How it works

One match goes through six steps. Each step passes its evidence to the next, so a claim on screen can be traced back to the events behind it.

  1. Events

    Passes, carries, shots, tackles and substitutions, with player positions at each moment.

  2. Detect

    Adds zones, progression, pressure and cover to every event.

  3. Tag

    Labels each event with its tactical reason: half-space entry, overload, a high press. The thresholds are tuned against planted truth.

  4. Test

    Moves the nearest teammate to four slots and ranks the chances, with the reasons and a calibration check.

  5. Narrate

    Writes a line for each event and a recap. Every claim links to the events it relies on.

  6. Curate

    Producers keep or drop each moment and export the kept list for a highlight package.

Who it is for

Producers and broadcasters

A ranked list of moments with a reason for each, ready to approve or drop.

Analysts and coaches

Shape, pressing and substitution reasons next to the 3D pitch, with an agent trace of how each label was made.

Fans

A plain-language reason for each key moment, with a glossary for xG, half-space, overload and press.

What it does not claim

  • The data is synthetic. Tactical labels agree with the generator, not with real football.
  • What-if values are model estimates, not predictions, and nothing here is betting or odds advice.
  • Substitution impact is descriptive, not a causal effect.
